# Weeks 4 and 5 Longitudinal Case Activities

## Govern Information, Then Make It Usable

This package continues Encounter 001 after transfer to the tertiary-care hospital. The health system can now receive outside information, but two questions remain:

1. Under what circumstances should people and systems access, exchange, or use the information?
2. How should appropriate information be matched to the correct patient and presented within clinical workflow?

The materials are organized as two independent modules. They can be delivered together during a shortened six-week course or separated when the course returns to seven weeks.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the activities, students should be able to:

1. Describe how purpose, identity, minimum necessary information, safeguards, and oversight influence access and use decisions.
2. Explain why protecting information and enabling appropriate exchange are both patient-safety responsibilities.
3. Identify patient-matching and reconciliation risks when external information enters an EHR.
4. Distinguish information that should be prominent, trended, available on demand, or accompanied by a warning.
5. Explain how provenance, timing, uncertainty, and workflow determine whether exchanged information is clinically useful.

## Module contents

- `shared_scenario.md` - common continuation of Encounter 001 and the health-system improvement project.
- `governance/` - an activity titled **Should This Sepsis Information Be Shared?**
- `ehr_workflow/` - an activity titled **Designing the Pharmacist's Sepsis Review**
- `slide_sequence.md` - slide-ready prompts for either a combined or separated delivery.

## Recommended timing

| Format | Governance module | EHR workflow module | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Combined opening activity | 15 minutes | 22 minutes | About 40 minutes with transitions |
| Separate class sessions | 20-25 minutes | 25-30 minutes | One module per session |

The activities are intended for guided discussion and participation. Students do not need to interpret statutes, configure an EHR, or produce a technically complete interface design.

All patients, organizations, systems, events, and identifiers in this module are synthetic. The governance scenarios are educational prompts, not legal advice or institutional policy.
