# Shared Scenario

## Encounter 001 After Transfer

The tertiary-care hospital received the patient from Encounter 001 and imported a transfer summary along with selected discrete results. The repeat lactate of 4.2 mmol/L reached the receiving organization after the patient departed the community hospital. The receiving team continued resuscitation, reviewed the antibiotic regimen, and admitted the patient to the ICU.

The health system's sepsis committee identifies two improvement opportunities:

1. **Organizational learning:** Review sepsis care across 100 synthetic encounters to understand delays, variation, and outcomes.
2. **Clinical workflow:** Create a pharmacist-facing sepsis review that brings together the information needed to assess antimicrobial therapy and the patient's response.

The ability to exchange information does not settle who should access it, how it may be used, or how it should appear in the EHR. The team must govern the information and make it usable without hiding uncertainty or creating new patient-safety risks.

## Available information

The health system may be able to retrieve:

- demographics and patient identifiers;
- encounter locations and timestamps;
- diagnoses and problem-list entries;
- vital signs and oxygen requirements;
- laboratory and microbiology results;
- medication orders and administrations;
- allergy entries;
- radiology reports;
- transfer and progress notes; and
- disposition and outcomes.

Some information was created locally, some came from the sending hospital, and some remains incomplete or internally inconsistent.

## Guiding question

> How can the health system make the right information available to the right people, for an appropriate purpose, in a form that supports safe action?
