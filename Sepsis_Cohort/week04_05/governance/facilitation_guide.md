# Governance Facilitation Guide

## Purpose

This activity introduces governance as a structured balancing task. Students should learn to ask the right questions, not memorize a single rule for every request.

## Recommended time

| Segment | Combined format | Standalone format |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Introduce scenario and framework | 3 minutes | 4 minutes |
| Groups evaluate request cards | 6 minutes | 10 minutes |
| Report out and compare | 4 minutes | 7 minutes |
| Debrief | 2 minutes | 4 minutes |

## Opening prompt

> Our systems can exchange more information than before. That technical capability creates a new responsibility: deciding who should receive which information, for what purpose, under what safeguards. Your task is to recommend a governed response, not to serve as legal counsel.

Assign one request card to each group. If there are fewer groups, prioritize Cards 1, 2, 3, and 5. If there are more groups, assign the same card to two groups and compare their conditions.

## Facilitation questions

- What purpose is the group trying to support?
- Did the group request more information than its purpose requires?
- Would the answer change if access were temporary rather than ongoing?
- Which uncertainty requires expert review?
- What would an audit record need to show?
- Did the group consider the patient-safety cost of withholding information?

## Expected observations by card

| Card | Expected emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Treating pharmacist | Timely treatment access, role relevance, and visible reconciliation of conflicting allergy information |
| Quality team | Fit between requested variables and measures, limited identifiers, retention, and recurring oversight |
| External vendor | Defined relationship, least access needed, time limitation, logging, supervision, and contractual safeguards |
| Research team | Research review pathway, authorization or other permission basis, reduced identifiers, linkage risk, and retention |
| Patient request | Identity verification, patient access, source labeling, portal limitations, and the risk of delay |
| External exchange | Treatment purpose, appropriate scope, genuine privacy/security concerns, and avoidance of inappropriate interference |

## Guardrails

Avoid treating a classroom recommendation as a definitive interpretation of HIPAA, the HITECH Act, the 21st Century Cures Act, or organizational policy. The lecture can connect student observations to those frameworks after students identify the practical governance questions.

## Transition to the EHR workflow module

> Suppose the request has been appropriately approved and the information reaches our organization. We still have to match it to the correct patient, preserve its meaning and source, and place it where a clinician can use it safely.
