# Governance Student Activity

## Should This Sepsis Information Be Shared?

Read `../shared_scenario.md`, then review the information request assigned to your group. Your task is not to make a definitive legal ruling. Identify the questions, safeguards, and tradeoffs the health system should consider before granting, limiting, or declining the request.

## Part 1 Evaluate the request

| Question | Group notes |
| --- | --- |
| Who is requesting the information? |  |
| What is the stated purpose? |  |
| Is the purpose connected to treatment, operations, payment, patient access, research, or another activity? |  |
| Whose information is needed? |  |
| Which specific data elements are needed? |  |
| Are direct identifiers necessary? |  |
| Is access one-time, time-limited, or ongoing? |  |
| Will the information remain inside the health system? |  |
| What agreement, authorization, review, or oversight may be needed? |  |
| How should access and use be logged or monitored? |  |

## Part 2 Recommend a response

Choose the response your group would recommend pending review by the appropriate privacy, security, compliance, or legal experts.

- Grant as requested
- Grant with limitations or safeguards
- Request additional information before deciding
- Decline or redirect the request

Describe the conditions you would attach:

1. Information included:
2. Information excluded or transformed:
3. People or systems permitted access:
4. Duration of access:
5. Safeguards, agreements, or oversight:
6. Audit or review plan:

## Part 3 Consider both kinds of risk

1. What harm could result from inappropriate access, disclosure, or use?
2. What harm could result from unnecessarily restricting or delaying appropriate access?
3. How does your recommendation balance these risks?

## Report out

In one minute, tell the class:

- the purpose of the request;
- your recommended response;
- your most important safeguard; and
- the most important unresolved question.
