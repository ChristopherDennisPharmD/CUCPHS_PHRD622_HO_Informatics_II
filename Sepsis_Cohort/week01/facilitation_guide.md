# Week 1 Facilitation Guide

## Purpose

This activity lets students experience an information-management problem before informatics terminology is introduced. The goal is not to test sepsis knowledge or reward the fastest chart reviewer. The goal is to make limitations in collecting, organizing, and interpreting clinical information visible.

## Recommended time

The core demonstration requires approximately 35 minutes.

| Segment | Time | Instructor action |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Introduce the health-system problem | 3 minutes | Explain that the hospital wants to understand whether deteriorating patients are recognized and treated promptly. |
| Fragmented chart review | 10 minutes | Assign one chart packet and the common worksheet to each group. |
| Group reporting | 8 minutes | Ask groups to report what they concluded, what remains uncertain, and where they found each fact. |
| Structured-summary reveal | 5 minutes | Display the organized view of the same encounters. |
| Informatics discussion | 9 minutes | Compare the experiences and connect them to the Pharmacists' Patient Care Process. |

## Opening prompt

Tell students:

> You are pharmacists asked to review a hospitalized patient whose condition may be changing. Use the available record to determine what is happening, what treatment has occurred, and what information still needs attention. You are not expected to diagnose the patient. Focus on what the record enables you to know.

Avoid defining informatics before the exercise. The initial difficulty provides the motivation for the definition.

## Facilitation guidance

During group work, respond to requests for missing information by asking students to record what they need and where they expected to find it. Do not resolve every ambiguity. Uncertainty is part of the demonstration.

When groups report, capture examples under four headings:

- Information that was easy to find
- Information that was difficult to find
- Information that appeared inconsistent
- Information that was unavailable or uncertain

## Structured-summary reveal

After the first discussion, display `structured_summary.md`. Give groups two minutes to revise their worksheet responses. Then ask:

1. Which questions became easier to answer?
2. Did the structured view change any conclusions?
3. What context was lost when the record was summarized?
4. Could a well-organized display still contain incorrect or incomplete data?
5. Who should decide what information is emphasized?

## Connection to course concepts

Close by connecting the activity to the Pharmacists' Patient Care Process:

- **Collect:** Fragmented sources increase search time and the risk of missing relevant information.
- **Assess:** Inconsistent timing, units, and documentation can change how the information is interpreted.
- **Plan and Implement:** Delayed recognition can delay treatment or communication.
- **Follow-up:** Trends and treatment responses are difficult to recognize without longitudinal organization.

Emphasize that informatics does not merely convert paper into electronic records. It examines how people, information, processes, and technology work together to support safe decisions.

## Transition to Week 2

End with the unresolved problem:

> Organizing information helped, but the records still used inconsistent names, abbreviations, units, and categories. Before a computer can reliably compare patients, how do we ensure that the data mean the same thing?

This question provides the transition to data standards.

## Participation evidence

Participation can be documented using three observable behaviors:

- The group identifies at least one important clinical-information limitation.
- The group explains how that limitation could affect patient care.
- The group contributes at least one improvement or tradeoff during discussion.

The activity is formative. It should not be graded for diagnostic accuracy.
