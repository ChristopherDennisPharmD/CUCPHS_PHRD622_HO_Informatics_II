# Week 1 Demonstration

## Introduction to Clinical Informatics

This demonstration introduces informatics through a practical question: how does the way clinical information is organized affect a pharmacist's ability to understand a patient's condition and act safely?

Students first review fragmented records from three synthetic hospital encounters. The activity is intentionally cumbersome, but it does not require technical expertise. After the group review, the instructor presents the same information in a structured summary so students can compare the two experiences.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the demonstration, students should be able to:

1. Identify ways fragmented clinical information can make patient assessment difficult.
2. Explain how information organization affects the Collect and Assess steps of the Pharmacists' Patient Care Process.
3. Describe benefits and limitations of presenting clinical data in a structured format.
4. Recognize that informatics involves people, processes, information, and technology rather than technology alone.

## Module contents

- `facilitation_guide.md` - suggested timing, prompts, and discussion structure.
- `student_worksheet.md` - questions completed by each student group.
- `chart_packets/` - three fragmented synthetic records for the initial review.
- `structured_summary.md` - organized view revealed after the initial activity.

## Suggested use

Divide the class into groups and assign one chart packet to each group. Multiple groups may review the same packet. Give students approximately 10 minutes to answer the clinical-information questions without expecting a complete clinical workup. After groups report their findings, reveal the structured summary and ask them to reconsider their answers.

All patients and events in this module are synthetic. The cases are instructional examples and are not clinical decision-support specifications.
