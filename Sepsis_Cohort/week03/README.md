# Week 3 Demonstration

## Data Standards Part 2

This demonstration continues Encounter 001 from Week 1. The patient is being transferred from a community hospital to a tertiary-care ICU, but relevant information remains distributed across several clinical systems and continues to change while the transfer is underway.

Students design the information handoff before learning the names or structures of specific interoperability standards. The activity establishes two distinct communication needs:

1. timely communication of individual events and discrete data; and
2. transfer of a coherent, persistent clinical summary with enough context for continued care.

The lecture can then introduce messaging standards and document standards as complementary approaches to these needs. Detailed exposure to HL7 v2 messages belongs in the separate post-lecture group activity.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the opening demonstration, students should be able to:

1. Describe why access to the same facts does not guarantee successful information exchange.
2. Distinguish time-sensitive event communication from transmission of a complete clinical summary.
3. Identify clinical information that may need to appear in both forms of communication.
4. Explain how missing structure, context, or workflow can make successfully transmitted information difficult to use.
5. Anticipate why healthcare systems need both messaging and document standards.

## Recommended time

The activity requires approximately 15 to 20 minutes. It can be shortened by assigning one complication to each group rather than presenting every complication sequentially.

All patients, organizations, systems, events, and identifiers in this module are synthetic.
