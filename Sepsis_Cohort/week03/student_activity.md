# Week 3 Student Activity

## Designing a Safe Information Handoff

Review `transfer_scenario.md`. Your group represents the receiving ICU team. Focus on what information you need, when you need it, and how it must be organized. You do not need to name or design a technical standard.

## Part 2 Design the handoff

1. Which information should reach the receiving organization as soon as an event occurs?

2. Which information should remain together as a complete clinical summary?

3. Which information belongs in both categories?

4. What context must accompany a laboratory value for the receiving team to interpret it correctly?

5. What distinctions must the medication information preserve?

6. What should happen when information changes after the transfer summary has been completed?

7. How should the receiving team know which organization or clinician is responsible for the information it receives?

## Part 3 Communication complication

Your instructor will provide one or more complication cards. For each complication, discuss:

1. What information was lost, delayed, or made difficult to use?
2. Can a clinician read the information?
3. Can the receiving computer use the information?
4. Does the problem involve content, structure, transport, patient matching, or clinical workflow?
5. What patient-care consequence could result?
6. What communication requirement would reduce the risk?

## Final reflection

Complete both statements:

> Some clinical information should be communicated as individual events because...

> Some clinical information should remain together as a complete summary because...
